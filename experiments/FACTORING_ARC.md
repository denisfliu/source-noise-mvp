# Factoring flow-matching action denoising into an orthonormal instruction subspace and a generative complement

Consolidated findings and reproduction guide. Method mathematics are in the
companion note (flow interpolation, orthonormal invariances, Fourier and graph
Laplacian from first principles, pass-through derivation, and the cross-body
invariance objective): https://claude.ai/code/artifact/2cbb1354-4674-4118-8a28-7c3df18adbbf

## 1. The construction

An action chunk is a sequence of `H` steps, each with `C` channels (degrees of
freedom), flattened to a vector `a` in R^D with `D = H*C`, expressed in a
canonical frame. A flow-matching model transports a Gaussian source `eps` to `a`
along the straight interpolation `x_t = (1-t) eps + t a`, learning the velocity
`a - eps`.

Choose an orthonormal matrix `U` (D-by-k, `U^T U = I_k`) whose columns are basis
vectors of a chosen basis. Define the instruction coordinate `c = U^T a`. Pin the
source by `eps' = eps + U (c - U^T eps)`, so that `U^T eps' = c`. Because `U` is
orthonormal, the source stays Gaussian off the subspace and the interpolation
keeps its form. During training `c = U^T a`, so `U^T x_t = c` for all `t` and the
velocity target has zero component on `U`. In the idealized limit the produced
action satisfies `U^T x_1 = c` as an identity (pass-through); in a trained model
the residual is small. The complement `(I - U U^T) x` is generated by the flow
conditioned on the observation and on the pinned coordinate.

This factors an action into (i) a low-dimensional coordinate `c` set from the
scene by a separate probabilistic model (a scene-to-coordinate prior), and (ii) a
generated complement. The coordinate is interpretable when the basis vectors have
a stated meaning, and controllable by pass-through. For cross-embodiment use, `U`
is chosen so `c` is determined by the task and not the body.

Bases used (all orthonormal, so pass-through holds):
- **Fourier**, eigenbasis of the periodic (cycle) Laplacian, per channel.
- **DCT / path-Laplacian**, eigenbasis of the path-graph Laplacian (free ends), per channel.
- **grid-Laplacian**, eigenbasis of the (time x channel) grid Laplacian with
  channel-edge weight `w`, whose low-eigenvalue modes couple channels and which is
  defined by a graph, so it is specified for any channel count without any learned quantity.
- **random**, a random orthonormal basis (control).
Selection within a basis is by a variance ratio (between-scene / within-group),
where the within group is either demonstrations (single body) or bodies (transfer).

## 2. Experiment ledger

Arms: `A`/`S` scratch flow (no pin); `F` Fourier pin; `DCT`, `GLAP` Laplacian
pins; `R` random pin; `*_oracle` uses the held-out item's true coordinate.
All success values are held-out, pooled over seeds. Executor is an autograd MLP
flow head; prior is a small MLP.

### 2.1 Planar obstacle reach, single embodiment (robosuite Panda)
Question: does a coherence-discovered structure help a policy-bottlenecked policy
at real kinematics scale?
Result (3 seeds): `A 0.021, F 0.383, Frand 0.008`; demo ceiling 0.82.
The scratch flow near-fails the detour; the discovered structure raises success by
about 0.36. Files: `experiments/rung2/collect_obstacle.py`, `structure_test.py`,
`structure_result.json`, `OBSTACLE_STRUCTURE_FINDING.md`.

discovered frame grows from 3 pins to 5, with lateral energy shifting to the

### 2.4 Scaling the pin to six degrees of freedom (robosuite Panda pose)

`C=6` (3 translation + 3 rotation), `D=192`.
- Pass-through binds at `D=192`: realized-minus-commanded coordinate error about
  0.01 to 0.04 against commands of magnitude about 13.
- Capacity sweep of scratch: success `0.10 / 0.75 / 0.95` at hidden width
  `128 / 256 / 384`. At the highest width the observation determines the answer,

R 0.233` (all near the floor). Files: `experiments/rung3/collect_pose6d.py`,
`structure_test_pose6d.py`, `pose6d_diag.py`, `pose6d_result.json`.

### 2.7 Steerability and interpretability at six DOF (Panda pose, coupled)
One flow executor with a grid-Laplacian pin (K=10). Interpretability: pinned

realized-minus-commanded slope is 1.000, and the produced behavior moves

### 2.8 Cross-embodiment transfer at six DOF (robosuite arms)
Coupled task collected on four arms (paired scenes by seed): demo ceilings
Panda 0.787, IIWA 0.654, UR5e 0.810, Jaco 0.738. Set-A = {Panda, IIWA, UR5e};
held-out = Jaco. Grid-Laplacian subspace (K=12) selected by cross-arm coherence
(top generalized eigenvalue about 746000), frozen; the set-A scene-to-coordinate
prior commands it; only the executor is relearned on Jaco.
Transfer success (3 seeds): `S 0.225, F 0.383, R 0.30, GLAP 0.75, GLAP_oracle
0.725`; Jaco's own demo ceiling 0.738. Cross-arm coordinate invariance:
GLAP 0.075, F 0.096, R 0.133.
The frozen task-space subspace plus a relearned complement raises held-out
success from 0.225 to 0.75, which is Jaco's native ceiling. The grid-Laplacian
exceeds Fourier and random, and the invariance ordering matches the success
ordering. Files: `experiments/rung3/transfer6d.py`, `transfer6d_result.json`.

### 2.9 Closed-loop execution in the simulator
Question: do the results hold when the generated chunk is executed under simulator
dynamics rather than integrated offline? A generated canonical pose-delta chunk is
integrated into an absolute pose waypoint sequence and tracked with the same OSC
position and orientation control used for collection; success is read from the
achieved trajectory.
Single arm, native training (Panda, coupled task) [closed-loop / offline]: scratch
`0.175 / 0.150`, grid-Laplacian `0.675 / 0.700`; per-scene agreement 0.925 for both.
Transfer arms (subspace frozen on set-A, complement relearned on the held-out arm,
executed on that arm) [closed-loop scratch / GLAP-transfer, offline scratch / GLAP,
agreement scratch / GLAP]:
Jaco `0.150 / 0.575`, `0.175 / 0.675`, `0.925 / 0.750`;
UR5e `0.325 / 0.575`, `0.275 / 0.750`, `0.850 / 0.825`.
The pin advantage holds under execution in both the single-arm and transfer settings.
The offline metric agrees with execution on 0.925 of scenes for a natively trained
policy; for a transferred policy the agreement is 0.75 to 0.825 and the offline metric
overstates success by about 0.1 to 0.18, a larger execution gap plausibly from
re-executing a trajectory whose complement was relearned on the held-out arm. Files:
`experiments/rung3/gen_cle.py`, `gen_cle_transfer.py`, `exec_cle.py`, `cle_result.json`,
`cle_result_Jaco.json`, `cle_result_UR5e.json`.

## 3. Synthesis

- Pass-through is a property of any orthonormal basis and holds at `D=192`. The
  mechanism factors an action into a pinned instruction coordinate and a generated
  complement.
- The pin improves task success only when the policy is the bottleneck. When the
  observation determines the action and the policy has enough capacity and data,
  the pin does not help and a mean-coordinate command can lower success.
- Steerability and interpretability do not depend on the bottleneck. At six DOF a
  single coordinate-space direction steers the coupled behavior through a sign
  change with realized-minus-commanded slope 1.000.
- Basis family matters in two regimes and not otherwise. For a single body with
  smooth structure, learning `U` is within noise of Fourier. Under a coupling in
  the task or a change of embodiment, a channel-mixing basis is needed and
  per-channel Fourier reaches scratch level. Among mixing bases, learning `U` is
  within noise of random in the toy transfer, whereas the structured
  grid-Laplacian exceeds random at six DOF with a coupled task, where the
  coordinate-invariance ordering also matches the transfer-success ordering.
- The task-space instruction subspace transfers across a change of arm to a
  held-out arm's native ceiling when only the complement is relearned.

- Closed-loop execution in the simulator confirms the six-DOF results, in the
  single-arm case with 0.925 per-scene agreement to the offline metric and in the
  transfer case with 0.75 to 0.825 agreement and a modest overstatement by the
  offline metric.

Caveats. Scratch baselines in the bottlenecked settings are low, so the size of the
lift depends on the bottleneck being present. The toy uses planted structure. All
embodiments tested so far share a six-DOF task-space action, so embodiment changes
with a different action dimension are not yet covered.

## 5. Generality across cross-embodiment situations

The transfer in Sections 2.8 and 2.9 assumes a shared task performed by different
bodies. That is one situation among several. This section reports the method across
four situations that differ in what changes between the source and the held-out
body, to establish where the strategy holds and where it does not, before any
physical-robot deployment.

### 5.1 The four situations
- Same task, different arm. Robots with different kinematics perform the same
  coupled task under the same six-channel end-effector pose interface (Section 2.8).
- Same arm, changed dynamics. One arm under the same interface with the low-level
  controller gain, damping, and latency changed, a stand-in for a change from a
  simulator to the physical system. Files: `rung3/collect_dyn.py`,
  `simreal_transfer.py`, `simreal_result.json`.
- Different action dimension. A six-channel pose embodiment and a three-channel
  position embodiment collected with different controllers, transferring a shared
  three-channel position instruction across the dimension change. Files:
  `rung3/collect_pos3.py`, `vardof_transfer.py`, `vardof_*_result.json`.
- Different task, different robot, on real data. Action chunks from four robots in
  Open X-Embodiment, reconstructing a held-out robot's chunks with a subspace fit
  on the others. Files: `rung3/oxe_extract.py`, `oxe_transfer.py`,
  `oxe_transfer_result.json`.

### 5.2 Ablation across situations
Held-out success by situation and method, pooled over three seeds, held-out set of
forty scenes. Deconvolution is the best value over the identification-data sweep;
the per-channel additive value was computed only for the cross-arm situation.

| situation | scratch | additive per-channel | additive cross-channel | deconvolution |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| same task, different arm (Jaco, 6-channel) | 0.225 | 0.383 | 0.750 | 0.14 |
| same arm, changed dynamics (6-channel) | 0.30 | not run | 0.700 | 0.975 |
| different action dimension (UR5e, 3-channel) | 0.808 | not run | 0.625 | 0.758 |
| different action dimension (Panda, 3-channel) | 1.00 | not run | 0.975 | 0.975 |

Column meanings: scratch is a flow policy with no pin; additive per-channel and
additive cross-channel are the pinned flow policy with a per-channel Fourier basis
and a channel-coupling grid-Laplacian basis; deconvolution identifies a per-channel
filter from a few held-out demonstrations and reconstructs the held-out trajectory
from the planned reference. Success is the offline geometric measure of Section 2;
the deconvolution and sim-to-real files hold the closed-loop check where available.

### 5.4 What holds and what does not
- No method has the lowest error or the highest success in every situation. The
  cross-channel additive pin is strongest for the same-task cross-arm situation, at
  0.750 against 0.14 for deconvolution, whereas deconvolution is strongest for the
  same-arm dynamics change, at 0.975 against 0.700. The cross-arm result shows
  deconvolution does not carry to a different arm, since it starts from a planned
  reference the held-out arm does not follow closely.
- For the different-action-dimension situation nothing exceeds scratch on UR5, since
  the additive pin falls below scratch and deconvolution lies between them and
  decreases as more demonstrations are used, and the Panda case is uninformative
  because scratch already reaches 1.0.
- On Open X-Embodiment the cross-channel grid-Laplacian reconstructs no better than
  a random subspace, consistent with the raw action chunks not sharing the channel
  coupling that the robosuite task imposed, and a subspace fit on other robots
  reconstructs the held-out robot worse than a fixed per-channel basis, consistent
  with data-driven directions being robot-specific. That measurement confounds the
  change of embodiment with the change of task, since the robots perform different
  tasks, so it is a structural probe rather than a controlled embodiment-transfer
  test.
- The strategy is reliable for two situations, transfer across arms performing the
  same task and transfer from a simulated body to the same body with changed
  dynamics, both holding the action interface fixed. It is not a single general
  cross-embodiment method: the cross-channel construction is specific to a task that
  couples channels, deconvolution is specific to a fixed body with changed dynamics,
  and the different-action-dimension and different-task situations are open.

`simreal_transfer.py`, `vardof_transfer.py` for the additive columns, and
`oxe_transfer.py` after `run_oxe_pipeline.sh` for the reconstruction study. Result
